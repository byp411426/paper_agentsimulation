## Abstract

1. Dynamic disaster response as an information, cognition, coordination, and
   execution process.
2. DisasterSociety purpose and the three-state separation.
3. EventPack, event-driven kernel, and intention–execution boundary.
4. Multi-scale, multi-evidence evaluation design.
5. Carr-informed controlled case and explicit non-claim of historical
   reconstruction.

   disruption, feedback, and re-planning.

4. Formal problem requirements: exposure-bounded, commitment-bounded,
   world-bounded.
5. Methodological challenges and evidence distinctions.
6. DisasterSociety solution overview.
7. Three contributions: platform, resident social-process model, and evaluation
   methodology.
